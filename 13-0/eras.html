The eras

Ninety years in the pool — and none of them equal.

1930s-40s

Bradman, Headley, Hammond — averages from another planet, but thin bowling cells outside England and Australia. The 40s are short (the war took the middle of it) and stacked with the Invincibles.

1950s-60s

The classical age: Sobers, Barrington, Trueman, Hall. Deep cells in every nation, spinners who bowled forever, and openers who could bat a day. Quietly the best value in the pool.

1970s-80s

Pace. Lillee and Thomson, then the four-pronged West Indies. If your tour is fast and bouncy, draft from here; if you face these decks, pick batters who proved themselves on them.

1990s-2000s

The modern golden age — Warne, Murali, McGrath, Ambrose against Lara, Tendulkar, Ponting, Kallis. The biggest cells, the most complete cricketers, the easiest place to fill a flexible slot.

2010s-2020s

The newest cards: Smith and Williamson with the bat, Bumrah and Boland with their absurd ratios, and the restored class of 2026. Short careers are damped, so trust the proven volume.
